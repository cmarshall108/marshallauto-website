During the hold, Dealer will not accept another person’s offer to buy this vehicle. Dealer may continue to show and advertise it and must disclose that it is subject to this hold. This agreement does not cover any other vehicle.

3. What each party must do before the hold ends

If Depositor wants to buy the vehicle, Depositor must, before the hold ends: (a) sign Dealer’s separate bill of sale or other sale documents; (b) pay the full agreed price, including taxes and official fees then due, in collected funds or complete financing acceptable to Dealer; and (c) satisfy every condition stated in those sale documents. Price, taxes, fees, and financing are agreed only in the separate sale documents. The asking price above is not an offer that Depositor can accept by paying this deposit.

Dealer is not obligated to sell the vehicle if, before the sale documents are signed, Dealer discovers a title, lien, odometer, safety, or other condition that prevents a lawful sale, or if Depositor does not meet the conditions above. Dealer will then return the deposit as provided below.

4. Deposit is earned unless Dealer cannot perform

Depositor agrees that Dealer gives up the chance to sell this vehicle to someone else during the hold, and that the deposit is a fair charge for that reservation—not a down payment and not earnest money. Dealer earns and may keep the entire deposit if the hold ends without a completed sale for any reason other than Dealer’s inability or refusal to complete a lawful sale on the terms later offered to Depositor. That includes Depositor changing their mind, not returning, not obtaining financing, not completing paperwork, or not paying on time.

Dealer will return the full deposit, with no deduction, within five (5) business days if: (a) Dealer sells or agrees to sell the vehicle to someone else during the hold; (b) Dealer cannot convey the title it represented, subject to disclosed liens and the stated title brand; or (c) Dealer otherwise refuses or is unable to complete a lawful sale. Return of the deposit is Depositor’s only remedy. Dealer is not liable for incidental, consequential, or lost-profit damages.

If a sale is completed, this deposit is credited against the sale price. It is not a separate fee. If the sale is later rescinded or the vehicle is returned under a separate written agreement, that agreement—not this form—controls what happens to the credited amount.

5. No warranty and no reliance

The vehicle is used and is offered “as is,” with all faults. Except for a title warranty that will appear only in a signed bill of sale, Dealer disclaims all express and implied warranties, including merchantability and fitness for a particular purpose, to the maximum extent allowed by North Carolina law. Nothing in this hold agreement limits a warranty that cannot legally be disclaimed. Depositor has had, or has knowingly declined, a reasonable opportunity to inspect the vehicle and have an independent mechanic inspect it. Any inspection must be finished before the hold ends. Oral statements, advertisements, and window writings are not part of this agreement.

6. Other terms

  • Depositor is at least 18 years old and is the person paying the deposit, or is authorized to bind the payer.
  • A card chargeback or reversed payment cancels the hold immediately, and Depositor remains responsible for the unpaid deposit.
  • Dealer may require a government-issued photo ID before accepting the deposit or releasing the vehicle.
  • This agreement is the entire agreement about the hold. It can be changed only by a written instrument signed by both parties. North Carolina law governs it. Venue lies in the state courts of Lee County, North Carolina.
  • If any provision is unenforceable, the rest remains in effect. In particular, if a court limits the amount Dealer may retain, Dealer may retain the greatest amount a court of competent jurisdiction allows.
  • Depositor may not assign this agreement. Dealer may assign it to a successor of the dealership.
